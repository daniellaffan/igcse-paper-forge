# Economics (International GCSE 4EC1-style): style guide

This guide is based on the private corpus (39 question papers, the matching mark schemes and the examiner reports, June 2019 to June 2025, including the R series). It is written in our own words. Nothing longer than a few words is quoted.

## Paper anatomy

| | Paper 1 | Paper 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Content | Microeconomics and business economics (spec topics 1–2) | Macroeconomics and the global economy (spec topics 3–4) |
| Time / marks | 1 h 30 min, 80 marks | 1 h 30 min, 80 marks |
| Calculator | allowed | allowed |
| Structure | 4 compulsory questions of 20 marks each | same |

- **Cover page.** It carries the usual rubric: black ink, fill in the candidate boxes, answer all questions in the spaces provided, calculators may be used. Information lines give the 80-mark total and say the marks in brackets are a guide to timing. Advice lines say to read each question, attempt every question and check your answers.
- **Page furniture.** Each page has a margin ("do not write in this area"). Every question ends with a "Total for Question N = 20 marks" line, and the last page says "TOTAL FOR PAPER = 80 MARKS".
- **MCQs.** They use a cross-in-the-box instruction, with a note that a change of mind is shown by a line through the box.

Each question runs from short recall items to an extended item, and usually stays within one topic area.

- **Q1 on Paper 1:** (a)(b) MCQ, define 2, state 1, define 1, calculate 2, diagram 3, explain 3, analyse 6.
- **Q1 on Paper 2:** MCQ ×2, define 2, describe 2, calculate 2, diagram 3, explain 3, analyse 6.
- **Q2 on Paper 1:** MCQ ×2, calculate 2, define 2, describe 2, explain 3, assess 9.
- **Q2 on Paper 2:** MCQ ×2, state 1, define 2, explain 3, diagram 3, assess 9.
- **Q3 on both papers:** MCQ ×2, diagram 3, then an analyse 6 and an assess 9 based on a short extract or figure.
- **Q4 on both papers (data response):** one or two extracts plus a figure (table or chart), then a calculation (2), an analyse (6) and an evaluate (12). This is modelled as one 20-mark `data-response` slot.

How the parts are spread by marks across the corpus: 1-mark 307, 2-mark 204, 3-mark 171, 6-mark 116, 9-mark 78, 12-mark 39.

**Difficulty ramp.** Within each question, difficulty rises from recall MCQs to applied 3-mark items to levels-marked writing. Calculations become harder when the student must first derive the percentage changes (PED from raw prices and quantities) or read a table.

**Contexts.** Real papers use short news-style extracts about real countries and industries. Our bank uses fictional firms and people placed in real cities for microeconomics, and fictional countries ("nations") for macroeconomic data. This means no invented statistics are attributed to real economies.

## Mark-scheme conventions

- **MCQ.** One correct letter, plus a line for each distractor explaining why it is wrong.
- **Point-marked items.** Each item has an answer, notes and marks. Notes cover accept/reject guidance, such as no marks for examples in definitions, only one point credited in "describe one…", and the third explain mark needing the context.
- **Calculations.** 1 mark for correct working using the data and 1 for the answer. A correct answer with no working gets 2. Writing a formula alone gets nothing. Units or currency are expected, and examiners penalise missing % signs and currency symbols.
- **Diagrams.** Each mark is for a labelled element. If both curves are shifted (or the wrong curve), no marks are given.
- **Levels grids.**
  - The grids are Level 0 to Level 3 with the bands 1–2/3–4/5–6, 1–3/4–6/7–9 and 1–4/5–8/9–12.
  - Descriptors move from isolated knowledge and asserted reasoning, to developed but partial chains, to coherent chains applied to the data. For 9 and 12 marks they also move to evaluation that is weighed and supported.
  - Indicative content is listed under AO2/AO3 (analysis) and AO4 (evaluation), and always ends with a line saying other valid points must be credited.
  - Marking is best fit.

## What examiner reports say

- Strong answers develop one or two points into full chains of cause and effect. Weak answers list several undeveloped points.
- Answers must use the data. Copying figures from the extract without explaining their significance is not analysis.
- Analyse questions do not need evaluation. Time spent on counter-arguments there is wasted.
- 9- and 12-mark answers often stay one-sided, or end with an unsupported "overall" judgement.
- Definitions are often vague, for example confusing demand with want, or using the term to define itself.
- On diagrams, students often forget to label the new curve, price or quantity, or they shift both curves.
- In calculations, students often omit units, round wrongly or confuse percentage change with absolute change.
- Students often confuse a shift of a curve with a movement along it.

## House rules for this bank

- Write a literal dollar sign as `\$`. Prefer £, €, ₹ and other currencies.
- Do not name the exam board in question text.
- All extracts are original and fictional, with the provenance line "Original fictional extract written for this question bank."
- Diagrams are original inline SVG drawn in currentColor.
