# Style guide: Biology 4BI1, separate science (Papers 1B and 2B)

This guide comes from reading the 48 question papers (1B, 1BR, 2B and 2BR, 2019–2025), their mark schemes, the examiner reports, the specification, the SAMs and the practical-terminology guide in the private corpus. Everything here is my own description, and nothing is quoted beyond a few words. In the bank, the R (alternative time-zone) papers are treated as the same format as 1B and 2B.

## 1. Paper anatomy

| | Paper 1B | Paper 2B |
|---|---|---|
| Time | 2 hours | 1 hour 15 minutes |
| Marks | 110 | 70 |
| Content | All non-bold specification content | All content, including the bold "B" (Biology-only) statements |
| Weighting | about 61% | about 39% |
| Questions | 9–11 numbered questions, each worth 4–16 marks | 6–7 numbered questions, each worth 6–18 marks |

- **No sections and no choice.** Every question is compulsory, and a calculator is allowed on both papers.
- **Cover.** The cover box lists a ruler and a calculator. The instructions say to use black ink, answer all questions in the spaces given, and show working. The information bullets give the paper total and note that marks are shown in brackets. The advice bullets say to read each question first, watch the time, and check answers.
- **Page furniture.** Each part shows its mark in brackets at the right. Each question ends with a "Total for Question n" line, and the paper ends with a paper total. Calculations have a dotted answer line with the unit printed after it. MCQs are answered by putting a cross in one box, and the paper tells candidates how to change an answer.
- **Ramp.** Each question starts with an AO1 recall part (an MCQ, "name" or "state") and then moves to application and data. The final Paper 1 question is often a plan-an-investigation question worth 5–6 marks.
- **Paper 2 Q1** is usually a long comprehension question (16–18 marks). It uses a printed article with numbered lines, and candidates answer from the passage plus their own knowledge. The bank builds it as the `comprehension` qtype (Paper 2 only): 12 original articles of 400–600 words in a numbered `passage` figure, each with 8–9 parts worth 1–3 marks (17 marks in total). The parts mix line-referenced recall, suggest, one MCQ, a calculation from figures in the text, own-knowledge explain and control or fair-test design. The first slot of the full 2B paper is this question.

| qtype | What it looks like | Part tariff | Command words |

**Difficulty.**
- Grades 1–3: recall and MCQ parts.
- Grades 4–6: single-link explanations and routine calculations.
- Grades 7–9: unfamiliar contexts, multi-step calculations (unit conversions, πr²l, 2ⁿ), probability chains (such as the 2/3 heterozygous-sibling question), plans, and Biology-only content.

## 3. How the mark scheme is written

- **Columns.** Every mark scheme uses four columns: question number, answer, additional guidance and mark.
- **MCQ rows** give the only correct answer with its letter and option text, then a short line for each distractor saying why it is wrong.
- **Point rows** start with "An explanation that makes reference to…" plus a number of points. The points are bullets, each ending in (1). An "any two from…" list is a single row whose mark is the number of points needed.
- **Additional guidance** sets out:
  - what to **accept** (synonyms, equivalent wording);
  - what to **reject** (wrong terms, such as "energy is made");
  - what to **ignore** (irrelevant additions);
  - when a point stands **alone** or depends on another;
  - **eq** (credit equivalent answers);
  - **allow ecf**, where a later mark can follow from an earlier wrong value.
- **Calculations** list the working steps as separate marks, then say that a correct answer with no working gets full marks. Rounding and significant figures are stated, and a unit is sometimes required for the last mark.
- **Plots** are marked on four things:
  - axes the right way round and labelled with units;
  - a sensible linear scale using at least half the grid;
  - points plotted within half a square;
  - a suitable line or curve.

  A bar chart is credited only for categoric data.
- **Plans** are point-marked on the CORMS headings:
  - **C**: change the independent variable over a range;
  - **O**: same organisms (or same age, size or species);
  - **R**: repeat;
  - **M1, M2**: measure the dependent variable, measure it over a set time;
  - **S1, S2**: same, i.e. two controlled variables.

  The mark is "six of the following". Live 4BI1 papers do **not** use levels grids, so the bank's `extended` qtype is point-marked: one 6-mark row listing creditable points, each worth (1), with the note "max 6". Calculation parts put their unit in `answer_suffix`.

## 4. What examiner reports say

- **Describe versus explain.** The most common lost marks come from describing a trend when the question asks for an explanation, or explaining when it asks for a description.
- **Quoting data.** Trend descriptions need data quoted with units. Strong answers also name the turning point or plateau.
- **Structure versus function.** Candidates mix the two, for example by giving the function of a part when asked how its structure differs.
- **Loose language is not credited**, for example:
  - "energy is produced";
  - "the enzyme is killed";
  - "the stomata breathe";
  - "the amount" without saying volume, mass or concentration.
- **Food chains.** Arrows must show the direction of energy flow, from the organism eaten to the eater.
- **Genetics.** Candidates often give a ratio when a probability is asked for. Gametes need to be shown clearly.
- **Calculations.** Working should be shown, because it earns method marks. Common errors are rounding too early and missing unit conversions (mm to µm, cm³ to dm³).
- **Plans.** Weak plans leave out repeats and named control variables, or describe a method that gives no measurement.
- **Layout.** Lists should be written one point per numbered line. Contradictory extra points cancel credit.

## 6. Bank conventions used here

- Mark points are written in my own words. Question text never names the board.
- Each question's papers and Paper 2-only flag come from its spec refs:
  - a question citing a bold "B" ref is set to `paper: ["2B"]` with `paper2_only: true`;
  - every other question is set to `["1B", "2B"]`.
- Every numerical answer is computed in the generator and stored in `answer.values` with `checked: true`. For graph and plot items, the values plotted are stored as well.
- Figures are black-and-white SVGs that use `currentColor`. Graphs have a minor grid. Diagrams use letter labels on leader lines. Punnett squares are tables, and pedigrees use the standard square, circle and shaded symbols.
