# History 4HI1: style guide (Dan's four options)

This guide comes from studying the private corpus: the specification (Issue 5), the specimen papers, and the question papers, mark schemes and examiner reports for June 2019 to November 2025. Everything here is my own description. Nothing longer than a few words is quoted.

Scope:
- Paper 1 depth studies 3 (Germany 1918–45) and 6 (A world divided 1943–72).
- Paper 2 Section A option A2 (Russia and the Soviet Union 1905–24).
- Paper 2 Section B option B1 (America 1783–1877).

## Paper anatomy

| | Paper 1 Depth Studies (4HI1/01) | Paper 2 Investigation and Breadth Studies (4HI1/02) |
|---|---|---|
| Time | 1 h 30 min | 1 h 30 min |
| Marks | 60 (50% of the qualification) | 60 (50%) |
| What candidates answer | Two depth-study questions out of the full list, each worth 30 marks | One Section A question (30 marks) and one Section B question (30 marks) |
| AO weighting | AO1 18%, AO2 22%, AO4 10% | AO1 16%, AO2 14%, AO3 15%, AO4 5% |
| Materials | A separate Sources/Extracts insert; answers go in a separate answer booklet | A separate Sources/Extracts insert; answers go in a separate answer booklet |

- **Cover rubric**, paraphrased:
  - Use black ink.
  - Answer the stated number of questions.
  - Answer EITHER (i) OR (ii) in part (c) where offered.
  - Write in the answer booklet.
  - The total is 60.
  - Marks are shown in brackets.
  - Read each question carefully and check your answers.
  - Spend about 45 minutes on each section or depth study.
- **Furniture:**
  - Each question carries a bold "Total for Question N" line.
  - Each paper ends with a total line and "END".
  - Extract and source labels give a short provenance line. Paper 1 extracts are attributed to a modern history book and a publication year. Paper 2 sources are attributed to a person or document type with a date.
- **Numbering:** questions are numbered by option on the real paper. For example, Germany is Question 3 on Paper 1, and Russia is Question 2 in Section A of Paper 2. Our assembler numbers questions in sequence instead.

## Question types

### Paper 1 (per depth study, 30 marks)
| Part | Tariff | AO | Stem pattern | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) | 6 | AO4 | Asks what impression the author gives about a topic, and requires candidates to use the named extract | One modern-historian extract of about 80–120 words. The best answers explain *how* the impression is created: selection, emphasis, language and omission. |
| (b) | 8 | AO1 4 + AO2 4 | Explain two effects of an event on a group, place or issue | No stimulus. Each effect must be developed with specific knowledge. |
| (c) | 16 | AO1 7 + AO2 9 | A quoted statement, followed by how far do you agree, two stimulus bullets and a demand for own knowledge | Candidates choose EITHER (i) OR (ii). The stimulus bullets usually give one point that supports the statement and one that does not. |

### Paper 2 Section A: historical investigation (30 marks)
| Part | Tariff | AO | Stem pattern | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) | 6 | AO1 | Describe two features of EITHER one topic OR another | Candidates answer on one topic. It is not linked to the sources. |
| (b) | 8 | AO3 | How far does Source A support the evidence of Source B about an issue | Two contemporary sources: a letter, a memoir, an official report, a newspaper or a speech. This is a cross-referencing task. |
| (c) | 16 | AO3 10 + AO4 6 | Extract C suggests a view; how far do you agree; use Extract C, Sources A and B and own knowledge | The extract is a modern interpretation. Sources A and B must be used as evidence for or against it. |

### Paper 2 Section B: breadth study in change (30 marks)
| Part | Tariff | AO | Stem pattern | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) | 6 | AO1 2 + AO2 4 | Explain two ways in which something at one time was different from it at another | Answers need explicit comparison. |
| (b) | 8 | AO1 4 + AO2 4 | Explain two causes of an event or development | Each cause must be linked to the outcome. |
| (c) | 16 | AO1 7 + AO2 9 | How far, or how significant, across a span of 25 years or more, with two stimulus bullets and own knowledge | EITHER (i) OR (ii). Questions usually cross at least two key topics, for example westward expansion and the road to civil war. |

**Difficulty ramp.** Within each 30-mark block, difficulty rises from (a) to (c). The 6-mark parts are accessible to grades 1–6. The 8-mark parts discriminate around grades 4–6. The 16-mark parts reward sustained analysis and a supported judgement, so Level 4 is grade 7–9 territory.

## How the mark schemes are written

- **Every part is levels-marked.** There are no point-marked items. Each grid gives a level, a mark band and a descriptor. The descriptors cover analysis, knowledge and (for the 16-mark parts) judgement.
- **Indicative content** is a bullet list. Paper 1 (a) pairs each impression with the extract wording that supports it. Section A (b) sets out agreement, then difference, then a comment on extent. Section A (c) sets out points that support the extract and points that challenge it. The list always ends with a reminder that it is not exhaustive and that other valid points earn credit.
- **Grids:**
  - 6-mark parts use three levels: 1–2, 3–4 and 5–6.
  - 8-mark parts use three levels: 1–2, 3–5 and 6–8.
  - 16-mark parts use four levels: 1–4, 5–8, 9–12 and 13–16.
- **Caps:**
  - In two-effect, two-cause and two-feature parts, an answer that covers only one item is capped. That is 4 marks on the 8-mark parts and 3 marks on the 6-mark parts, and such an answer cannot reach the top level.
  - On the 16-mark parts, an answer that does not go beyond the two stimulus points is capped at 6 marks in Level 2 and 10 marks in Level 3. Level 4 needs three or more aspects, including own knowledge.
  - In Section A (b), the top of Level 2 needs both agreement and difference.
- **Marking guidance:**
  - Examiners first decide the level, then the mark within it.
  - They are told to give credit for valid points not listed.
  - In Section A (b), reliability is explicitly not required.

## What examiner reports say

- **Paper 1 (a):** many answers simply paraphrase the extract or use own knowledge, and stay in Level 2 or below. Strong answers name a clear impression and then comment on the author's choices: tone, emphasis, loaded words and what is left out.
- **(b) effects and causes:**
  - Weak answers describe the event.
  - Strong answers state the outcome and explain the link, with precise detail such as dates, figures and names.
  - Spending too long on (b) and leaving too little time for (c) is a common timing problem.
- **16-mark parts:**
  - Strong answers state a view early and set criteria, such as duration, breadth of impact or whether other factors depended on this one.
  - They bring in at least one factor beyond the bullets and reach a judgement that follows from the argument.
  - One-sided answers and lists of factors without weighing stall in Level 2 or 3.
- **Section A (b):**
  - Candidates lose marks by evaluating each source's reliability rather than comparing content.
  - Strong answers match specific details across the sources and comment on how far the sources agree overall.
- **Section A (c):**
  - The extract cannot be used to prove itself.
  - Support and challenge must come from the sources and from own knowledge.
  - Answers that ignore own knowledge are limited.
- **Section B (a):**
  - Answers that describe each period separately without comparing them stay low.
  - Comparative words such as "whereas" help.
- **Section B (c):** candidates often take too short a view. For example, they write about 1850s events only when the question spans 1820–61.

## Generator choices that follow from this

- Each Paper 1 subtopic has at least 12 original extracts or extract foci, 12 effects items and 12 judgement statements.
- Russia uses clusters of four original sources with tagged claims. These generate cross-reference indicative content automatically, giving agreement, difference and extent. Each cluster also has two original extracts with three views each.
- America uses difference, cause and factor banks for each key topic.
- All sources and extracts are invented and period-accurate, and each carries provenance.
