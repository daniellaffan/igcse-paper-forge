# Style guide: Cambridge IGCSE Spanish (Foreign Language) 0530

Based on the 2021–2025 corpus: question papers, mark schemes, listening transcripts, the speaking teacher notes and role-play cards, examiner reports, the 2021 specimen papers, and the 2025–2027 syllabus (664626) with its update notice (683348). Everything below is my own description. Quotations are kept to a few words at most.

## Overall shape

There are four separately entered components. All carry equal weight (25% each):

| Paper | Code | Time | Marks | Form |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Listening | 0530/11–13 | about 50 min, incl. 6 min to transfer answers | 40 | 37 items, all selected response, answered on a lozenge answer sheet |
| 2 Reading | 0530/21–23 | 1 h | 45 | six questions in a fixed order |
| 3 Speaking | 0530/03 | about 10 min plus 10 min preparation | 40 | role play + two topic conversations, conducted by the teacher, moderated |
| 4 Writing | 0530/41–43 | 1 h | 45 | three questions; Q3 is a choice of two tasks |

Every rubric is in Spanish and every answer is written in Spanish. Dictionaries are not allowed. The cover pages carry the usual Cambridge furniture:
- candidate name, number and centre boxes;
- a short list of instructions (answer all questions, use the answer spaces, write in Spanish);
- information lines (the total mark, marks in square brackets);
- a page count and copyright footer, with blank pages marked as such.

Listening papers also say that each text is heard twice and that there is transfer time at the end.

## Paper 1: Listening (fixed order every series)

| Qs | Task | Marks | Our qtype |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–8 | Eight short one- or two-line utterances set in a single scenario (a friend, a visit, a shop). Four picture options A–D; one key word decides it | 8 | `l-short` |
| 9–14 | A short announcement or guide's talk with one pause; six A–D items on facts, times, places and rules. Options are short phrases or pictures | 6 | `l-announce` |
| 15–19 | Five young people speak in turn about one theme; match each to one of six paraphrased statements (one is spare) | 5 | `l-match` |
| 20–28 | An interview in two parts; nine three-option items (A–C), with the order following the recording | 9 | `l-interview-3` |
| 29–34 | A longer interview with a pause; six four-option items that need inference and opinion | 6 | `l-interview-4` |
| 35–37 | An interview in three sections; for each section, choose two true statements from five (2 marks each) | 6 | `l-multi` |

- **Stems.** The stems are short Spanish questions or sentence starts that end in a colon. Distractors reuse words from the recording.
- **Paraphrase.** The right answer is usually a paraphrase, never a lift.
- **Difficulty.** It rises through the paper, from naming a concrete noun to judging attitude and reasons.
- **Transcripts.** Every line is introduced by a speaker label, and the instruction says each text is heard twice.
- **No production.** No item asks the candidate to write Spanish. In the current format there is no gap-fill and no true/false-with-correction.

## Paper 2: Reading (fixed order)

| Q | Task | Marks | Our qtype |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Five short sentences matched to pictures (one picture extra) | 5 | `r-pictures` |
| 2 | Public signs or notices in one setting; five sentences describe a need, and the candidate matches each to the sign | 5 | `r-signs` |
| 3 | A personal e-mail or message of about 150–180 words; seven A–C items | 7 | `r-text-mcq` |
| 4 | A first-person blog or account; short answers in Spanish | 12 | `r-short` |
| 5 | Five people describe what they want; match each to one of eight adverts, with near-miss adverts as decoys | 5 | `r-match-ads` |
| 6 | A longer interview-style article; short answers in Spanish, including inference | 11 | `r-long` |

- **Answer format.** Short-answer stems are third-person questions about the writer. Answers are a few words.
- **Getting harder.** Later items need the candidate to switch the verb from first to third person, to pick one detail out of a list, and to resist copying a nearby distractor.
- **Answer lines.** Answer lines are printed with a mark in brackets.

## Paper 3: Speaking

- **Preparation.** The candidate gets 10 minutes to prepare one role-play card without notes. The test begins with a short unmarked greeting.
- **Role play, about 2 minutes, 10 marks.** The card sets a scene (a shop, station, doctor, host family) and names the teacher's role. There are five tasks marked 2/1/0. Task 4 is always in the past and task 5 in the future or conditional. Some tasks have two halves, with a pause after the first. The teacher reads each question exactly as printed. It may be repeated once but never reworded.
- **Two topic conversations, 4 minutes each, 30 marks.**
  - Conversation 1 comes from Areas A/B and conversation 2 from C/D/E, allocated by a randomisation grid.
  - Each has five scripted questions moving from present, to opinion with *¿Por qué?*, to past, to future or conditional. Questions 3–5 have an alternative wording.
  - If a conversation runs short, up to two extra questions on the same topic may be asked.
  - Both conversations are marked together on two 15-mark grids: Communication, and Quality of Language. Each grid has bands 13–15, 10–12, 7–9, 4–6, 1–3 and 0.
- **Bank format.** The bank stores the role play (`s-roleplay`) as one item and the two conversations (`s-conversation`) as one paired item, with the teacher script in the stem.

## Paper 4: Writing

| Q | Task | Marks | Our qtype |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Complete a form: about five labelled gaps (one may ask for two items); single words or short phrases | 5 | `w-form` |
| 2 | 80–90 words; four bullets (one an opinion with *¿Por qué?*, so five tasks); a single 12-mark grid | 12 | `w-short` |
| 3 | Choose (a) an e-mail or letter, or (b) a blog or article; 130–140 words; five bullets spanning present, past, opinion and future/conditional | 28 = Task completion 10 + Range 10 + Accuracy 8 | `w-extended` |

The bullets are written to the candidate as tú-form questions.

## How the mark schemes are written

- **Listening.** A key of letters. The two-from-five items show both letters ("D / E"), one mark each, with a deduction where extra letters are marked.
- **Reading.**
  - The scheme is a table of item, answer, notes and marks.
  - Underlining marks the required element and round brackets mark optional words. A slash separates alternatives.
  - Notes say what to accept and what to refuse. Notations include *INV* (extra material makes the answer wrong), *tc* (too close to the text, or not enough on its own), *HA* (hostile alternative) and occasional *ORA*.
  - Tolerance is for communication. Minor spelling slips are accepted unless they make another Spanish word.
  - A first-person lift is refused where it changes the meaning.
- **Writing Q1.** Gap-by-gap Accept and Refuse lists. Any word that fits the label scores, and the mark is for communication.
- **Writing Q2/Q3 and Speaking.** Level grids are applied to the whole response: read, choose the band, then top or bottom of the band. Task completion depends on the right time frame for each bullet and needs a correct verb. Material that does not answer the bullets is ignored, and a wholly irrelevant answer scores 0.
- **Role play.** 2 marks for a complete message (small slips allowed), 1 for partial or ambiguous, and 0 otherwise. A short answer can earn 2.

## What the examiner reports keep saying

- **Person.** Reading answers about someone else need the third person. Copying *tengo* or *mi* instead of *tiene* or *su* loses marks when meaning shifts.
- **Over-lifting.** Copying a whole sentence often drags in a wrong detail (INV). Strong candidates write short, precise answers.
- **Accents that change meaning or tense.** The commonest are *enseño/enseñó*, *trabajo/trabajó*, *esta/está* and *el/él*.
- **Confusable forms.** Weak scripts mix up *por qué* and *porque*, *me gusta* and *me gustaría*, *ser* and *estar*, and preterite and imperfect.
- **Writing.** Every bullet must be addressed in the time frame it asks for. Answers that ignore the past or the future bullet cap task completion. Rehearsed paragraphs that do not answer the bullets get little credit. Going far beyond the word count does not help.
- **Speaking.** The best candidates develop answers with reasons, examples and linked sentences without being prompted. Weak candidates give one-word answers or need the alternative question. Teachers who rephrase role-play questions, or add extra prompts beyond the script, disadvantage candidates.
- **Listening.** Candidates who pick the option that repeats a word they heard fall into the distractor. Successful candidates listen for meaning.

The P1 papers print only questions, not transcripts, and P4 papers are short, so their counts understate what is tested. The pattern is still clear:
- Family, friends, leisure, home, school and the natural world dominate.

- Speaking conversations draw on education and family in almost every series.

## Rules our generator follows

- **One task block per bank question.** Each question covers one task block as it appears on the paper, with its real mark total.
- **Listening.** Original transcripts are written one utterance per line as "Name: text", with times written in words. Distractors come from the same recording or a related near-miss. Pictures are written as English captions, e.g. "[picture: apples]", as a stand-in for drawings.
- **Reading texts.** All are original and in a single consistent first-person voice. Short-answer mark schemes give Accept/alternatives and a Refuse note (usually the first-person lift or the nearest distractor).
- **Speaking and writing.** These use paraphrased versions of the real grids, with indicative content for each bullet or question tagged by time frame.
- **Language level.** All Spanish is kept at IGCSE level: present, preterite and imperfect, near and simple future, conditional, a few common subjunctives (*para que*, *cuando* + subjunctive) and standard topic vocabulary from the syllabus list.
