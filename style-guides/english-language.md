# Style guide: Pearson Edexcel International GCSE English Language A 4EA1, Paper 1

Scope: Paper 1 (Non-fiction Texts and Transactional Writing) only. Based on the private corpus: the specification, the sample assessment materials, Paper 1 question papers, source booklets and mark schemes from June 2018 to June 2025 (including 01R variants) and the examiners' reports. Everything here is described in our own words; nothing longer than a few words is quoted.

## 1. Paper anatomy

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Code | 4EA1/01, with a paired 4EA1/01R paper for another timezone (different texts and tasks) |
| Time | 2 hours 15 minutes |
| Marks | 90 (60% of the qualification) |
| Rubric | Answer ALL questions in Section A and ONE question in Section B |
| Section A | Reading, 45 marks, suggested about 90 minutes. Text One is an unseen non-fiction extract; Text Two is an extract from Part 1 of the Anthology |
| Section B | Transactional writing, 45 marks, suggested about 45 minutes. Two tasks, EITHER/OR |
| Booklets | Question paper plus a separate source (extracts) booklet that prints both texts with line numbers every five lines and a short italic introduction. Answers are written in the question paper |
| Furniture | Paper reference and "Turn over" on each page, "(Total for Question n = X marks)" after each question, section totals, a reminder at the end of Section A to move on, and an advice box saying marks for writing reward both content and accuracy (SPaG) |

## 2. Question types

### Section A (Text One, unseen)
* **Q1 (2 marks, AO1).** Look at a short line range (2-4 lines) and select two words or phrases that describe or show something. One mark per correct selection; the mark scheme lists acceptable words, with optional words in brackets. No explanation is needed.
* **Q2 (4 marks, AO1).** Look again at a longer range (about a paragraph or two) and, in your own words, describe or explain something (what happens, what we learn about a person or place). One mark per valid, distinct point. Points must be in the candidate's own words; lifted phrases are not credited.
* **Q3 (5 marks, AO1).** From a range of lines, explain the writer's thoughts/feelings or what we learn about something; candidates may support points with brief quotations. One mark per valid point (with or without quotation), so five points earns full marks.

### Section A (Text Two, anthology)
* **Q4 (12 marks, AO2).** How does the writer use language and structure to present/show/convey a focus (a person, an experience, a place, the writer's feelings)? Marked in five levels (1-2, 3-4, 5-7, 8-10, 11-12) with indicative content listing language, structural and tonal features.
* **Q5 (22 marks, AO3).** Compare how the writers of Text One and Text Two present ideas and perspectives about a shared theme (danger, challenges, a place, people they meet, growing up). Five levels (1-4, 5-8, 9-13, 14-18, 19-22). Answers using only one text are capped in Level 2. Indicative content lists similarities and differences of idea, perspective and method.
* The two texts are always paired thematically: the unseen text is chosen to echo the anthology extract (for example, a modern adventure piece with 127 Hours, a travel piece with the Polo extract).

### Section B (transactional writing, 45 marks)
* Two tasks; candidates choose one. Each task sets a form, audience and purpose, often with a short prompt or quoted statement, and two bullet points the candidate "may" use, followed by "and any other ideas you may have". A reminder to write for the stated audience follows.
* Forms in the corpus (Paper 1 and 01R, counted): article 13, letter 10, speech 6, guide 5, leaflet 3; reviews are named in the specification and appear rarely. Typical contexts: school life and rules, technology and young people, the environment, travel and holidays, sport and health, community events, money and work.
* Marked against AO4 (27, levels 1-5, 6-11, 12-17, 18-22, 23-27: communication, form, tone, register, organisation) and AO5 (18, levels 1-3, 4-7, 8-11, 12-15, 16-18: vocabulary, sentence structure, spelling and punctuation).

### Difficulty ramp
Q1 is accessible retrieval; Q2 and Q3 grow in demand with longer ranges and abstract tasks (feelings, attitudes). Q4 and Q5 discriminate most: Q5 is the hardest because it needs integrated comparison of both content and method. Section B demand comes from the task's audience and the control of register, not the topic.

## 3. Mark scheme conventions
* Q1-Q3: point lists (no levels). Q1 lists acceptable selections and notes that the whole sentence copied, or words outside the range, are not credited. Q2/Q3 list indicative points, with "any four/five" style rubric and a note to accept other valid interpretations.
* Q4, Q5 and Section B: levels grids (level, mark band, descriptor) applied on a best-fit basis, with indicative content that is "not prescriptive". Examiners are told to start at the level that best fits, then move within the band by the strength of evidence.
* AO2 descriptors move from identification and limited comment, through some understanding and appropriate references, to perceptive, sustained analysis with discriminating references. AO3 descriptors move from describing one or both texts to a varied and perceptive comparison with a balanced range of examples.

## 4. What the examiners' reports say
* Q1: stay strictly within the given lines; one word or short phrase each; explanations are unnecessary and copying whole sentences wastes time.
* Q2: four separate points; candidates who quote rather than paraphrase, or who drift into commenting on language (AO2), lose marks. Using lines outside the range is a common error.
* Q3: brief quotations help but are optional; long quotations or repeated points lose marks. Strong answers make five distinct, inferential points.
* Q4: strong answers link a feature to its effect in relation to the focus, cover structure (openings, shifts, endings, sentence variety) and not only vocabulary. Weak answers spot features ("uses a simile") without comment or retell the extract.
* Q5: the main discriminator is genuine comparison of ideas, perspectives and methods across both texts; writing about each text separately or ignoring Text Two limits the level. Strong answers use connectives of comparison throughout.
* Section B: plan; match register to audience; use form conventions (headlines and subheadings for articles, salutation and sign-off for letters, direct address for speeches); develop the bullets rather than listing them. Accuracy of basic punctuation (sentence demarcation, apostrophes) and paragraphing separates bands.

## 5. Topic frequency (anthology texts on Paper 1, 01 and 01R papers counted where the title appears)
127 Hours 3; Explorers or boys messing about? 3; Young and dyslexic? 3; A Game of Polo with a Headless Goat 3; Beyond the Sky and the Earth 3; H is for Hawk 3; Chinese Cinderella 2; The Danger of a Single Story 2; The Explorer's Daughter 1; A Passage to Africa 0 in the papers counted (the title may be printed only in source booklets not in the corpus). Every anthology text is in scope, and our bank covers all ten with four Q4 focuses each.

## 6. Conventions used in our bank
* Unseen passages are original, by invented writers, 650-800 words, with manual line numbers every five lines. Anthology extracts are never reproduced; the item names the text and author and asks the student to use their own copy.
* Stems are paraphrased from the real formulas, never copied. Grids, best-fit guidance and SPaG notes are written in our own words.
* Section A appears as one combined item (parts a-e) on a single Text One/Text Two pairing; Section B as one EITHER/OR item.
