### english-literature.unseen-brothers-room.0003  (20 marks)
Read the following poem.
My Brother's Room
Since you went to university
your room has held its breath.
The posters stay. The football boots
still sulk beneath the bed.

Mum hoovers it on Thursdays
though nobody has walked there.
She opens the window, says
it's for the air.

I go in when no one's looking.
I sit on your desk chair
and spin it slowly round
the way you used to, when we'd argue

about whose turn it was
on the computer, or the bathroom,
or who had eaten the last
of the cereal. I'd give

all the cereal in the world
to have you bang on the wall now
and tell me to turn the music down.
Instead I turn it up. Just to hear the quiet.

### english-literature.unseen-lighthouse.0001  (20 marks)
Read the following poem.
The Lighthouse Keeper's Daughter
I grew up inside a candle
on a rock the size of a church.
Every night my father climbed
one hundred and twelve steps
to light the sea.

I learned my letters from the logbook:
WIND NW 6. FOG. ONE SHIP.
My friends were gulls with names,
a seal that came on Thursdays,
the slow tick of the lamp.

On the mainland, children ran
in gangs along the harbour wall.
I watched them through the glass
like a film with the sound off,
and wondered what they laughed at.

Now I live in a city
where the lights never go out,
and nobody looks up at them,
and some nights I climb the stairs
to my flat, counting: one hundred and twelve.
