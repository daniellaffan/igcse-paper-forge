### english-literature.unseen-grandmothers-hands.0003

    LLevel 2 5-8: A largely descriptive response with some comment on the writer's language, form and structure. Some relevant examples are used in support.
    LLevel 3 9-12: Shows understanding of a range of the writer's choices of language, form and structure and links them to their effect on the reader. Example
    LLevel 4 13-16: A focused, detailed response that sustains analysis of the writer's language, form and structure and their effect on the reader. Examples ar
    LLevel 5 17-20: A cohesive evaluation of how the writer's language, form and structure work together and affect the reader. Examples are chosen with discrim
    • **Focus:** The final image of the hands that 'know / the shape of mine' suggests a deep, almost instinctive bond between grandmother and grandchild.
    • **Descriptive skills:** The hands are described through an extended metaphor of a map: veins become 'blue roads', knuckles 'little hills' and the scar 'a river', so the body becomes a record of a lifetime.
    • **Descriptive skills:** Small, precise details such as the scar from the kitchen knife make the portrait feel real and personal rather than general.
    • **Descriptive skills:** The second stanza builds a picture of hard physical work (kneading, wringing sheets in frost) so that the reader admires the grandmother's strength.
    • **Language choices:** A sequence of active verbs ('kneaded', 'wrung', 'pinned', 'smoothed') shows constant labour and care; the phrase 'never once stood still' sums up her energy.
    • **Language choices:** The simile 'like two birds come home to roost' suggests rest, return and perhaps the approach of the end of life, treated gently rather than sadly.
    • **Language choices:** The idiom 'learning me by heart' is a pun: the hands have memorised the speaker, and the word 'heart' links this memory to love.
    • **Form and structure:** Three seven-line stanzas move clearly through past (the map of a life), work (the hands in the past) and present (the hands at rest).
    • **Form and structure:** Free verse with short lines and frequent enjambment gives a calm, reflective, conversational voice.
    • **Form and structure:** The tense shifts from past to present in the final stanza, and the ending reverses the roles: the hands that once cared for the child are now held by the grandchild.
