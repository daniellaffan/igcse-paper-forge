### history.russia-support.0040  (8 marks)
Source A: From a letter written by a peasant to a newspaper, December 1917.
At last the land is ours! The new government has given us the landlord's estate without payment. We have divided it fairly by the number of mouths in each family. The Red Guards in town have shut the newspaper of the Socialist Revolutionaries, but what do we care for newspapers? Long live the Soviet power!
Source B: From a report by an official of the Cheka, 1918.
In the past month we have arrested 400 counter-revolutionaries in this province, including priests, former officers and speculators. Forty have been shot. The bourgeoisie must learn that the dictatorship of the proletariat is not a joke.
 Study Sources A and B.
How far does Source A support the evidence of Source B about the Bolshevik consolidation of power in 1917–18?
Explain your answer. [8 mark(s)]

### history.russia-support.0017  (8 marks)
Source A: From a police report on the mood in Petrograd, October 1916.
The price of bread has risen fourfold since the start of the war. Women stand in queues all night and often go home with nothing, while every week brings new lists of the dead from the front. Workers speak openly against the Tsar and the Tsarina. There is a real danger that hunger will lead to riots.
Source B: From an official army newspaper, 1916.
Our brave troops under General Brusilov have broken the Austrian lines and taken hundreds of thousands of prisoners. The war demands great sacrifices from every family, but new factories now supply shells in great numbers. The nation stands united behind its Emperor, who shares the hardships of his soldiers at the front.
 Study Sources A and B.
How far does Source A support the evidence of Source B about conditions in Russia during the First World War?
Explain your answer. [8 mark(s)]

### history.russia-support.0048  (8 marks)
Source A: From a letter written by a British officer serving with the Whites, 1919.
Denikin's army is advancing fast and may reach Moscow before winter. The Reds are falling back in disorder, and the people in the towns we take greet us with relief. With more supplies from Britain we could finish the Bolsheviks this year.
Source B: From the diary of a railway worker in central Russia, 1920.
Trotsky's armoured train passed through again today, full of commissars and machine guns. The Reds hold all the railway lines from Moscow, and they move their men wherever they are needed. They take our grain and shoot anyone who hides it, but at least they are not the landlords, and not the English and French who are paying the Whites.
 Study Sources A and B.
