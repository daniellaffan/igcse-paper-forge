    • **AO2 – *Search For My Tongue*:** The speaker imagines having two tongues in one mouth, an extended metaphor for living between two languages.
    • **AO2 – *Search For My Tongue*:** The fear that the mother tongue will 'rot' and be spat out conveys anxiety about losing one's culture.
    • **AO2 – *Search For My Tongue*:** The Gujarati lines in the centre of the poem show the language physically on the page, so form becomes part of the meaning.
    • **AO2 – *Search For My Tongue*:** Plant imagery of roots, veins, buds and blossom presents the language as alive and able to regrow.
    • **Second poem:** candidates may choose any poem from the anthology that allows a valid comparison. Suitable choices might include *Blessing* (which shows a community's shared identity in a moment of collective joy), *Prayer Before Birth* (which fears that the modern world will destroy the individual identity of the child), *Half-caste* (which challenges a label that defines a mixed-heritage identity as incomplete).
    • **AO2 – *Blessing* (if chosen):** The community is described as a whole crowd surging out with pots, pans and buckets, suggesting a shared identity.
    • **AO2 – *Prayer Before Birth* (if chosen):** The unborn child fears being turned into a mere part of a machine, losing human identity.
    • **AO3:** Both poems are concerned with the importance of language, but *Search For My Tongue* presents identity as rooted in a mother tongue that seems to rot away but grows back stronger, whereas *Blessing* shows a community's shared identity in a moment of collective joy.
    • **AO3:** *Search For My Tongue* is free verse divided by a central passage in Gujarati script with a phonetic transliteration, while *Blessing* is free verse in irregular stanzas, opening with a very short line; candidates may compare how each form shapes the presentation of the importance of language.
    • **AO3:** The voice of *Search For My Tongue* is a first-person speaker addressing the reader directly in a conversational way; in *Blessing* it is an observer describing a community in a third-person, present-tense narrative, which changes how directly the reader is involved.
    • **AO3:** The tone of *Search For My Tongue* is anxious at first, then triumphant, compared with the celebratory but aware of hardship tone of *Blessing*.
    • **AO3:** Whichever poem is chosen, the best answers keep both poems in view throughout and explain how the writers' methods create different effects.
- general: Mark with the levels grid using a best-fit approach. AO2 and AO3 are equally weighted. Reward comparison that is developed and supported by close reference to both poems; the comparison may be integrated throughout or organised poem by poem with explicit links. Summary or feature-spotting without comment on effect should not be highly rewarded.
