# english-literature — mark scheme for batch english-literature-000

### english-literature.anth-open-advice.0004
- () levels-marked: AO2 Analyse the language, form and structure used by a writer to create meanings and effects (15 marks); AO3 Explore links and connections between texts (15 marks)
    L0 0: No rewardable material.
    LLevel 1 1-6: A simple response with minimal identification of language, form and structure. Little or no comparison of the two poems. Little relevant evidence.
    LLevel 2 7-12: A largely descriptive response with some comment on language, form and structure. Some undeveloped comparison, noting obvious similarities and/or differences. Some relevant examples. NB: an answer that considers only ONE poem cannot go above the top of Level 2.
    LLevel 3 13-18: Shows understanding of a range of language, form and structure, linked to effects on the reader. Compares and contrasts a range of points, considering some similarities and/or differences. Clearly relevant examples.
    LLevel 4 19-24: Focused and detailed, with sustained analysis of language, form and structure and their effects. Compares and contrasts effectively, across a wide range of similarities and/or differences. Fully relevant examples.
    LLevel 5 25-30: A cohesive evaluation of how language, form and structure work together to affect the reader. Perceptive comparison covering a varied and comprehensive range of similarities and/or differences. Discriminating use of examples.
    • **AO2 – *Prayer Before Birth*:** The prayer form gives the poem the seriousness of a religious appeal.
    • **AO2 – *Prayer Before Birth*:** The child's list of fears forms a powerful criticism of war and society.
    • **AO2 – *Prayer Before Birth*:** The final line presents the most extreme view: better not to live than to lose humanity.
    • **Second poem:** candidates may choose any poem from the anthology that allows a valid comparison. Suitable choices might include *Sonnet 116* (which makes a firm argument about what true love is), *Remember* (which offers advice to a loved one about how to grieve), *Half-caste* (which argues strongly against prejudice through mockery and wit).
    • **AO2 – *Sonnet 116* (if chosen):** The speaker presents his definition as fact, beginning with a refusal to admit impediments.
    • **AO2 – *Remember* (if chosen):** The speaker first asks to be remembered, then advises her loved one not to be sad.
    • **AO3:** Both poems are concerned with attitudes towards life, but *Prayer Before Birth* expresses a strong point of view about the dangers of the modern world, whereas *Sonnet 116* makes a firm argument about what true love is.
    • **AO3:** *Prayer Before Birth* is a prayer in stanzas that lengthen and then collapse into a single final line, while *Sonnet 116* is a Shakespearean sonnet of three quatrains and a closing couplet in iambic pentameter; candidates may compare how each form shapes the presentation of attitudes towards life.
    • **AO3:** The voice of *Prayer Before Birth* is an unborn child praying to be protected from the world; in *Sonnet 116* it is a confident first-person speaker defining true love, which changes how directly the reader is involved.
    • **AO3:** The tone of *Prayer Before Birth* is urgent, fearful and pleading, compared with the assured, argumentative and serious tone of *Sonnet 116*.
    • **AO3:** Whichever poem is chosen, the best answers keep both poems in view throughout and explain how the writers' methods create different effects.
- general: Mark with the levels grid using a best-fit approach. AO2 and AO3 are equally weighted. Reward comparison that is developed and supported by close reference to both poems; the comparison may be integrated throughout or organised poem by poem with explicit links. Summary or feature-spotting without comment on effect should not be highly rewarded.
