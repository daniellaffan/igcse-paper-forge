### english-literature.unseen-drought.0001  (20 marks)
Read the following poem.
Drought
For seven months the sky has been
a closed door. The river shrank
to a brown thread, then to a scar.

The cattle stand in what shade there is
and do not move their tails.
The maize is paper. The well is a throat.

In the evenings my father walks the fields
and comes back with dust in his eyebrows,
saying nothing, and my mother serves

the same thin soup and nobody complains.
On the radio, a man in the city
says the rains will come next month, or next.

Tonight, though, the dog lifted her nose
and the air turned, and the first cloud
came over the hill like a rumour,

and all of us, even my father,
went out into the yard and stood there,
faces up, like seeds.

### english-literature.unseen-exam-hall.0001  (20 marks)
Read the following poem.
Exam Hall
Two hundred desks in rows like graves,
a clock that clears its throat each minute,
the smell of floor polish and fear.

The invigilator walks the aisles
in soft shoes, a heron in a cardigan.
Somebody's pen is clicking. Stop. Stop.

I read the first question three times
and each time it is in a different language.
My heart is a bird inside a box.

Outside, a lawnmower drones
across the playing field, and someone
is shouting a dog's name, over and over,

and the world is going on
as if this were not the hinge
on which my whole life turns.

Then I pick up the pen.
I know this. I do.
The words come walking back like cattle.

### english-literature.unseen-first-frost.0001  (20 marks)
Read the following poem.
First Frost
Overnight the garden has been
silvered, every blade of grass
a needle stitched with white.

The spider's web on the gate
is a hammock strung with salt;
the bird bath is a locked eye.

I step out and the lawn
crunches like a biscuit,
my breath a ghost that runs ahead.

Even the noisy blackbird
has nothing to say,
puffed up on the frozen wall.

By ten the sun will find it all
and take it back, drop by drop,
as if the night had never written

this one cold page
and left it on the doorstep
for me alone to read.
