### english-literature.unseen-ninety-candles.0002  (20 marks)
Read the following poem.
Ninety Candles
We could not fit ninety on the cake
so we put on nine, one for each ten years,
and he blew them out in three goes,
laughing at himself, wheezing like a kettle.

He has outlived his brothers, two wives,
a war, a dog called Smudge, the trams,
the shop on Station Road, and most
of the people in his photographs.

He tells the same four stories.
We let him. Each time he tells them
they grow a little taller, like children,
and he grows a little smaller in his chair.

When we sing, he conducts us
with a fork, very seriously,
as if we were an orchestra
and this were the last night of the proms.

Afterwards he falls asleep, cake on his tie,
and his great-granddaughter, who is two,
climbs up and sleeps on him,
the oldest and the newest of us, breathing in time.

### english-literature.unseen-old-photograph.0001  (20 marks)
Read the following poem.
Old Photograph
Here are my parents before me,
squinting into a sun
that set forty years ago.

He has hair. She is laughing
at something outside the frame,
something I will never know.

Behind them, a sea I've never seen
holds still, its one wave
frozen white for ever.

They do not know about the rent,
the redundancy, the hospital,
or me. They are not waiting

for anything. They are just
two people on a wall
at the edge of an ordinary day,

and I would give a great deal
to tell them nothing,
to let them stay exactly there.

### english-literature.unseen-ozymandias.0002  (20 marks)
Read the following poem.
Ozymandias
I met a traveller from an antique land,
Who said - "Two vast and trunkless legs of stone
Stand in the desert .... Near them, on the sand,
Half sunk a shattered visage* lies, whose frown,
And wrinkled lip, and sneer of cold command,
Tell that its sculptor well those passions read
Which yet survive, stamped on these lifeless things,
The hand that mocked them, and the heart that fed;
And on the pedestal, these words appear:
My name is Ozymandias, King of Kings;
Look on my Works, ye Mighty, and despair!
Nothing beside remains. Round the decay
Of that colossal Wreck, boundless and bare
The lone and level sands stretch far away." 

*visage: face

*Percy Bysshe Shelley (1818)*
