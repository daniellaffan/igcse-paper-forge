### history.russia-investigation.0044
- (b) levels-marked: Cross-reference

    • Difference: Source B suggests that the Whites were strong and dangerous (Denikin's army is advancing fast and may reach Moscow), which Source A does not mention.

    LLevel 2 5–8: A developed answer that uses the extract, the sources and some own knowledge to comment on the interpretation, but may be one-sided. A judgement is given but is only partly supported.
    LLevel 3 9–12: An analytical answer that considers evidence both for and against the interpretation from the sources and own knowledge. The judgement is linked to the evidence but is not fully sustained.

    • Challenge from Source B: it suggests that the Whites were strong and dangerous (Denikin's army is advancing fast and may reach Moscow).

    • Own knowledge supporting the interpretation: The Reds could use the railways to move troops.

    • Own knowledge challenging the interpretation: The Whites also had poor leadership and unity.
    • Own knowledge challenging the interpretation: Red propaganda and terror were important.
    • Own knowledge challenging the interpretation: Trotsky's discipline in the Red Army.

- general: (b): This question tests AO3 (analysing and comparing sources). Comments on the reliability or provenance of the sources are not required and gain credit only where they are used to explain the extent of support. (c): The extract cannot be used to support itself: support or challenge must come from Sources A and B and own knowledge. Answers that use only the extract and sources, with no own knowledge, are limited to Level 2. A clear judgement on how far the candidate agrees should be stated and sustained.
