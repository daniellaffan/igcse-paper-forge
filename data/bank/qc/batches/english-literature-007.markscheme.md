### english-literature.unseen-a-birthday.0002
- () levels-marked: AO2 Analyse the language, form and structure used by a writer to create meanings and effects (20 marks)
    L0 0: No rewardable material.
    LLevel 1 1-4: A simple response that identifies very little of the writer's use of language, form and structure. Little relevant evidence is used in suppo
    LLevel 2 5-8: A largely descriptive response with some comment on the writer's language, form and structure. Some relevant examples are used in support.
    LLevel 3 9-12: Shows understanding of a range of the writer's choices of language, form and structure and links them to their effect on the reader. Example
    LLevel 4 13-16: A focused, detailed response that sustains analysis of the writer's language, form and structure and their effect on the reader. Examples ar
    LLevel 5 17-20: A cohesive evaluation of how the writer's language, form and structure work together and affect the reader. Examples are chosen with discrim
    • **Focus:** Natural images (a singing bird, a laden apple-tree, a shell in a calm sea) express fulfilment and contentment.
    • **Descriptive skills:** The first stanza describes nature at its most abundant and peaceful.
    • **Descriptive skills:** The second stanza describes a luxurious, richly decorated platform for a celebration.
    • **Descriptive skills:** Colours and materials (purple, gold, silver, silk) make the second stanza lavish and royal.
    • **Language choices:** Anaphora of 'My heart is like' builds a sequence of similes, each more joyful.
    • **Language choices:** 'My heart is gladder than all these' shows that no comparison is enough to express her feelings.
    • **Language choices:** Imperatives in the second stanza ('Raise', 'Hang', 'Carve', 'Work') show excitement and urgency.
    • **Language choices:** The metaphor 'the birthday of my life' suggests that love has given her a new life.
    • **Form and structure:** Two eight-line stanzas: the first natural, the second artificial and decorative.
    • **Form and structure:** Each stanza ends with the same idea, 'my love is come to me', which acts as a refrain.
    • **Form and structure:** Regular rhythm and rhyme give the poem the quality of a song of celebration.
- general: Mark with the levels grid using a best-fit approach, then use the indicative content as a guide to what candidates may say. Reward points that are firmly grounded in the poem and show some personal response. Summary, paraphrase or a list of techniques without comment on effect should not be highly rewarded.
