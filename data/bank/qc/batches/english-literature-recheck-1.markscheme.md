# english-literature — mark scheme for batch english-literature-recheck-1

### english-literature.omam-setting.0012
- () levels-marked: AO1 Demonstrate a close knowledge and understanding of texts, maintaining a critical style and presenting an informed personal engagement (20 marks); AO4 Show understanding of the relationships between texts and the contexts in which they were written (20 marks)
    L0 0: No rewardable material.
    LLevel 1 1-8: Limited knowledge and understanding of the text. A simple response with little personal engagement or critical style. Little comment on the relationship between text and context. Limited use of relevant examples.
    LLevel 2 9-16: Some knowledge and understanding of the text. Largely narrative, with some personal engagement or critical style. Some comment on the relationship between text and context. Some relevant examples.
    LLevel 3 17-24: Sound knowledge and understanding of the text. Relevant personal engagement and an appropriate critical style. Relevant comment on the relationship between text and context. Clearly relevant examples.
    LLevel 4 25-32: Thorough knowledge and understanding of the text. Thorough personal engagement and a sustained critical style. Detailed awareness of the relationship between text and context. Fully relevant examples.
    LLevel 5 33-40: Assured knowledge and understanding of the text. Assured personal engagement and a perceptive critical style. Understanding of the relationship between text and context is convincingly integrated into the response. Discriminating use of examples.
    • **Approach:** candidates may agree, disagree or argue a balanced case; any judgement is acceptable if it is supported by close reference to the novel.
    • **AO1:** The novel is in six sections, each set mainly in one location like scenes in a play.
    • **AO1:** The circular structure begins and ends at the pool.
    • **AO1:** Tension builds through a series of incidents that foreshadow the climax.
    • **AO1:** Calm descriptions of nature open sections and contrast with the violence that follows.
    • **AO1:** Dialogue drives much of the story, showing characters' attitudes directly.
    • **AO1:** The final scene repeats the dream, linking the beginning and the end.
    • **AO4:** Steinbeck designed the novel as a 'play-novelette', with scenes set in a small number of locations and much of the story told through dialogue; it was staged as a play in the same year it was published.
    • **AO4:** The title alludes to Robert Burns's poem 'To a Mouse' (1785), which reflects that the most careful plans of mice and men often go wrong; this frames the novel's sense of fate from the start.
    • **AO4:** Steinbeck had himself worked on ranches in the Salinas Valley and drew on his observations of the men he met; he wanted to give a voice to the people who were invisible to wealthier Americans.
- general: Mark with the levels grid using a best-fit approach. AO1 and AO4 are equally weighted. Reward knowledge of the whole novel, a clear line of argument that answers the question, and context that is woven into the discussion rather than added on. Retelling the story without argument should not be highly rewarded. This is a closed-book paper, so close reference to events may be as effective as quotation.

### english-literature.unseen-brothers-room.0003
- () levels-marked: AO2 Analyse the language, form and structure used by a writer to create meanings and effects (20 marks)
    L0 0: No rewardable material.
    LLevel 1 1-4: A simple response that identifies very little of the writer's use of language, form and structure. Little relevant evidence is used in support.
    LLevel 2 5-8: A largely descriptive response with some comment on the writer's language, form and structure. Some relevant examples are used in support.
    LLevel 3 9-12: Shows understanding of a range of the writer's choices of language, form and structure and links them to their effect on the reader. Examples are clearly relevant.
    LLevel 4 13-16: A focused, detailed response that sustains analysis of the writer's language, form and structure and their effect on the reader. Examples are fully relevant.
    LLevel 5 17-20: A cohesive evaluation of how the writer's language, form and structure work together and affect the reader. Examples are chosen with discrimination.
    • **Focus:** The brother's departure has changed the household, shown in the mother's cleaning routine and the room that 'has held its breath'.
    • **Descriptive skills:** The empty room is described through objects left behind: posters and football boots.
    • **Descriptive skills:** The mother's routine of cleaning the unused room is a small detail that reveals her feelings indirectly.
    • **Descriptive skills:** The speaker's own secret visits to the room are described in a quiet, physical way.
    • **Language choices:** Personification ('your room has held its breath', boots that 'sulk') suggests that the room itself is waiting for the brother.
    • **Language choices:** The mother's excuse 'it's for the air' is understated and touching.
    • **Language choices:** The list of past arguments is humorous and realistic, contrasting with the sadness of absence.
    • **Language choices:** The final paradox 'turn it up. Just to hear the quiet' shows that loud music only makes the brother's absence clearer.
    • **Form and structure:** The poem is addressed to 'you', the absent brother, like a letter.
    • **Form and structure:** Some quatrains have loose rhyme ('there / air'), but the form loosens as memory takes over, reflecting emotion.
    • **Form and structure:** Enjambment over stanza breaks ('I'd give // all the cereal in the world') builds to the emotional climax in the final stanza.
- general: Mark with the levels grid using a best-fit approach, then use the indicative content as a guide to what candidates may say. Reward points that are firmly grounded in the poem and show some personal response. Summary, paraphrase or a list of techniques without comment on effect should not be highly rewarded.

- () levels-marked: AO2 Analyse the language, form and structure used by a writer to create meanings and effects (20 marks)
    L0 0: No rewardable material.
    LLevel 1 1-4: A simple response that identifies very little of the writer's use of language, form and structure. Little relevant evidence is used in support.
    LLevel 2 5-8: A largely descriptive response with some comment on the writer's language, form and structure. Some relevant examples are used in support.
    LLevel 3 9-12: Shows understanding of a range of the writer's choices of language, form and structure and links them to their effect on the reader. Examples are clearly relevant.
    LLevel 4 13-16: A focused, detailed response that sustains analysis of the writer's language, form and structure and their effect on the reader. Examples are fully relevant.
    LLevel 5 17-20: A cohesive evaluation of how the writer's language, form and structure work together and affect the reader. Examples are chosen with discrimination.
    • **Focus:** The lighthouse is described as a strange, isolated home where the speaker's whole childhood was shaped by the sea.
    • **Descriptive skills:** The lighthouse is pictured in surprising ways: as 'a candle' and on 'a rock the size of a church'.
    • **Descriptive skills:** The father's routine is shown in a precise detail: climbing 'one hundred and twelve steps'.
    • **Descriptive skills:** The city in the final stanza is described in contrast, where the lights 'never go out' and that nobody notices.
    • **Language choices:** The metaphor 'I grew up inside a candle' suggests both warmth and fragility.
    • **Language choices:** The capitalised logbook entries create a realistic, clipped voice and show the child's unusual education.
    • **Language choices:** The list of animal 'friends' (gulls, a seal 'that came on Thursdays') is touching and slightly humorous.
    • **Language choices:** The simile 'like a film with the sound off' conveys distance and exclusion.
    • **Form and structure:** Four five-line stanzas move from childhood to adulthood.
    • **Form and structure:** The repeated number 'one hundred and twelve' connects the opening and ending, showing that the past still shapes the speaker.
    • **Form and structure:** The final stanza shifts to the present with 'Now', inviting the reader to see the speaker as still lonely in a crowded city.
- general: Mark with the levels grid using a best-fit approach, then use the indicative content as a guide to what candidates may say. Reward points that are firmly grounded in the poem and show some personal response. Summary, paraphrase or a list of techniques without comment on effect should not be highly rewarded.
