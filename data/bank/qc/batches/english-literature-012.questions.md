### english-literature.unseen-hope-feathers.0002  (20 marks)
Read the following poem.
'Hope' is the thing with feathers
'Hope' is the thing with feathers -
That perches in the soul -
And sings the tune without the words -
And never stops - at all -

And sweetest - in the Gale - is heard -
And sore must be the storm -
That could abash* the little Bird
That kept so many warm -

I've heard it in the chillest land -
And on the strangest Sea -
Yet - never - in Extremity*,
It asked a crumb - of me.

*abash: embarrass or silence
*Extremity: the worst of times

*Emily Dickinson (written c.1861; published 1891)*

### english-literature.unseen-hospital-window.0001  (20 marks)
Read the following poem.
Hospital Window
For three weeks the window was my world:
one tree, a car park, a slice of sky
the shape of a slice of bread.

I learned the tree like a face.
Its leaves went from green to rust to gone.
A crow came every morning at ten

and stood on the same branch, inspecting me.
The nurses changed. The car park filled
and emptied like a lung.

Some days the sky was the only thing moving.
Some days I was too tired to look,
and the window looked at me instead.

Today they wheeled me out
through doors that sighed open by themselves,
and the wind hit me like a slap of cold water,

and I cried, not because I was sad,
but because the sky was suddenly
the size of everything again.

### english-literature.unseen-innisfree.0001  (20 marks)
Read the following poem.
The Lake Isle of Innisfree
I will arise and go now, and go to Innisfree,
And a small cabin build there, of clay and wattles* made;
Nine bean-rows will I have there, a hive for the honey-bee,
And live alone in the bee-loud glade.

And I shall have some peace there, for peace comes dropping slow,
Dropping from the veils of the morning to where the cricket sings;
There midnight's all a glimmer, and noon a purple glow,
And evening full of the linnet's* wings.

I will arise and go now, for always night and day
I hear lake water lapping with low sounds by the shore;
While I stand on the roadway, or on the pavements grey,
I hear it in the deep heart's core.

*wattles: woven sticks used for building
*linnet: a small songbird

*W. B. Yeats (1890)*
