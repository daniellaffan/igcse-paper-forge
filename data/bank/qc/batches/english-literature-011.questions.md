### english-literature.unseen-futility.0002  (20 marks)
Read the following poem.
Futility
Move him into the sun -
Gently its touch awoke him once,
At home, whispering of fields half-sown.
Always it woke him, even in France,
Until this morning and this snow.
If anything might rouse him now
The kind old sun will know.

Think how it wakes the seeds -
Woke once the clays of a cold star.
Are limbs, so dear-achieved, are sides
Full-nerved, still warm, too hard to stir?
Was it for this the clay grew tall?
- O what made fatuous* sunbeams toil
To break earth's sleep at all?

*fatuous: pointless or foolish

*Wilfred Owen (written 1918; published 1918)*

### english-literature.unseen-grandmothers-hands.0003  (20 marks)
Read the following poem.
Grandmother's Hands
My grandmother's hands are maps
of every place they've been:
the blue roads of her veins,
the knuckles' little hills,
a white scar like a river
where a kitchen knife once slipped
the winter she was twelve.

They kneaded bread at dawn
for seven hungry children,
they wrung out sheets in frost
till the skin split at the tips,
they pinned my first school badge
and smoothed my hair down flat,
and never once stood still.

Now they rest on the blanket
like two birds come home to roost,
thin and light as paper.
When I hold them, they still know
the shape of mine exactly,
as if, all along, they had been
learning me by heart.

### english-literature.unseen-heron.0001  (20 marks)
Read the following poem.
Heron
Grey as the rain it waits in,
the heron stands on one leg
at the river's edge, so still
the water forgets it is there.

It is a folded umbrella,
a thin old man in a coat,
a question nobody asked.
Minutes pass like barges.

Then the neck unlocks.
The beak goes in like a knife
through silver paper, and comes out
with a fish, a flicker, gone.

And the heron is still again,
as if nothing had happened,
as if the river had only blinked
and the morning had only breathed.
