### english-literature.unseen-storm-estuary.0003  (20 marks)
Read the following poem.
Storm over the Estuary
All afternoon the sky has been
gathering itself like a fist.

The gulls go first, inland, crying,
then the light, then the far shore.

Now the wind arrives on the mud
and drags the water up in sheets,

the moored boats tug and rear
like dogs that want to be let off.

Thunder rolls its barrels
down the stairs of the hills.

I should go in. I stay
with my back against the sea wall,

soaked, laughing, small,
while the whole grey world

throws itself against the stone
and does not break it, quite.

### english-literature.unseen-street-cricket.0002  (20 marks)
Read the following poem.
Street Cricket
A crate for stumps, a plank for a bat,
a ball gone bald with hitting,
and the whole lane is a stadium

from the tailor's step to the water pump.
Uncles stop their scooters to advise.
Grandmothers shout from balconies

that the ball is nearly in their washing.
Six! someone yells, though the ball
has only bounced off the baker's shutter.

We argue about every run like lawyers.
We play till the light goes orange,
then grey, then the ball is a guess

and our mothers' voices come
calling us home one by one,
until only the crate is left, still standing,

its three painted lines
waiting for tomorrow, like a promise
that the lane will keep.

### english-literature.unseen-suitcase.0002  (20 marks)
Read the following poem.
Suitcase
My grandfather left his country
with one suitcase. He told me
what was in it, so often
that I can pack it in my sleep:

two shirts, a razor, a photograph
of a woman I would later call Nani,
a book of poems in a script
I cannot read, a handful of earth

tied up in a handkerchief.
At the border they asked him
what the earth was for.
He said, To remember where I'm from.

The soldier laughed and let it go.
It is in a jar on our mantelpiece now,
beside the clock and the wedding photo,
a little hill of brown dust.

Sometimes I hold it up to the window
and try to see a field in it,
a river, a house, a door
that no key in this country opens.
