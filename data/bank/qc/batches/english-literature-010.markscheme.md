### english-literature.unseen-first-frost.0001
- () levels-marked: AO2 Analyse the language, form and structure used by a writer to create meanings and effects (20 marks)
    L0 0: No rewardable material.
    LLevel 1 1-4: A simple response that identifies very little of the writer's use of language, form and structure. Little relevant evidence is used in suppo
    LLevel 2 5-8: A largely descriptive response with some comment on the writer's language, form and structure. Some relevant examples are used in support.
    LLevel 3 9-12: Shows understanding of a range of the writer's choices of language, form and structure and links them to their effect on the reader. Example
    LLevel 4 13-16: A focused, detailed response that sustains analysis of the writer's language, form and structure and their effect on the reader. Examples ar
    LLevel 5 17-20: A cohesive evaluation of how the writer's language, form and structure work together and affect the reader. Examples are chosen with discrim
    • **Focus:** The frost transforms an ordinary garden, and each object (grass, web, bird bath) is shown changed by the cold.
    • **Descriptive skills:** Visual detail dominates: the grass is 'silvered', the web 'strung with salt', creating a delicate, glittering scene.
    • **Descriptive skills:** Sound is used sparingly: the lawn 'crunches' and the blackbird is silent, emphasising the stillness of the frozen morning.
    • **Descriptive skills:** The speaker's own body enters the scene through the image of breath as 'a ghost that runs ahead'.
    • **Language choices:** Metaphors drawn from sewing and the home ('a needle stitched with white', 'a hammock') make nature seem crafted and careful.
    • **Language choices:** The simile 'crunches like a biscuit' is homely and slightly playful, contrasting with the more mysterious images.
    • **Language choices:** The bird bath as 'a locked eye' is an unsettling image suggesting that nature has closed itself off in winter.
    • **Language choices:** The final extended metaphor treats the frost as writing on 'this one cold page', linking nature to reading and poetry.
    • **Form and structure:** Short three-line stanzas (tercets) present one image at a time, like a series of snapshots.
    • **Form and structure:** The poem moves from early morning to the predicted thaw 'By ten', giving it a clear time structure.
    • **Form and structure:** Enjambment across the last two stanzas makes the final sentence run on, slowing the ending and stressing 'for me alone'.
