    • **Focus:** The child's fear of sinking is overcome only through trust in the parent's voice and hand.
    • **Descriptive skills:** The physical sensations of learning to swim are described closely: the roar of water in the ears and the ceiling lights that 'swam'.
    • **Descriptive skills:** The parent is described through actions (the flat hand, the 'arms half-raised / in case') rather than appearance.
    • **Descriptive skills:** The final picture of the parent's face, 'proud and frightened', is described from the child's point of view.

    • **Language choices:** Short imperatives in direct speech ('Lie back', 'Breathe') show the parent's calm control.
    • **Language choices:** The phrase 'a wall of water' emphasises how distant and frightening the world feels to the child under the water.
    • **Language choices:** The paradox of being 'proud and frightened / at the same time' captures the parent's mixed feelings.
    • **Form and structure:** The poem is addressed to 'you', the parent, so it reads like a thank-you or memory shared directly.
    • **Form and structure:** Short sentences 'I breathed. I floated.' show the moment of success simply and calmly.
    • **Form and structure:** The final stanza is a single isolated line, 'at the same time', which leaves the reader with the parent's mixed emotion.
