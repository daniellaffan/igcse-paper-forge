### history.russia-support.0017  (8 marks)
Source A: From a police report on the mood in Petrograd, October 1916.
The price of bread has risen fourfold since the start of the war. Women stand in queues all night and often go home with nothing, while every week brings new lists of the dead from the front. Workers speak openly against the Tsar and the Tsarina. There is a real danger that hunger will lead to riots.
Source B: From an official army newspaper, 1916.
Our brave troops under General Brusilov have broken the Austrian lines and taken hundreds of thousands of prisoners. The war demands great sacrifices from every family, but new factories now supply shells in great numbers. The nation stands united behind its Emperor, who shares the hardships of his soldiers at the front.
 Study Sources A and B.
How far does Source A support the evidence of Source B about conditions in Russia during the First World War?

### history.russia-support.0007  (8 marks)
Source A: From the memoirs of a liberal member of the First Duma, published in 1922.
When the Manifesto was published in October 1905, we believed that Russia had become a constitutional state at last. Our joy did not last. Within months the Tsar had issued laws that left him master of the army, the ministers and the purse. When we criticised his government, he simply sent us home.
Source B: From a letter written by an army officer serving in Moscow, December 1905.
We have cleared the Presnya district street by street. The workers fought well behind their barricades, but they could not stand against artillery. Hundreds are dead. The Manifesto satisfied the lawyers and professors, and so the workers fought alone. The Tsar owes his throne to the army.
 Study Sources A and B.
