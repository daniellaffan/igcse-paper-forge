    • **AO1:** Crooks is the Black stable hand; he lives alone in a room by the barn and is excluded from the bunkhouse because of his race.
    • **AO1:** His room contains books and his own possessions, suggesting intelligence, pride and a degree of permanence other workers lack.
    • **AO1:** He is initially hostile when Lennie enters his room, defending the only space where he has rights.
    • **AO1:** He torments Lennie by suggesting George might not come back, which reveals his own loneliness and bitterness.
    • **AO1:** He speaks of his childhood on his father's chicken ranch and of the loneliness of having no one to talk to.
    • **AO1:** Briefly he asks to join the dream farm, but withdraws after Curley's wife threatens him, showing how quickly hope is crushed.

    • **AO4:** Ranch life was strictly hierarchical: the boss and his family held power over hired hands, who had no union protection and could be 'canned' with no notice.
    • **AO4:** The American Dream promised that anyone who worked hard could own property and be independent; for ranch workers in the 1930s this dream was almost impossible to achieve, which gives the novel its tragic irony.
